# Japanese counter words

Japanese counts things with counter suffixes: long objects with 本, flat things with 枚, small animals with 匹, people with 人. Origa treats each suffix as one card and trains two kinds of knowledge inside it.

## One suffix is one card

A counter card carries the suffix itself (what it counts) and the number bindings: the readings of one through ten, the question form 何, and a few fixed forms beyond ten (the days of the month like 二十日, read はつか). You meet the card through the acquaintance stage like any other new card: the presentation slide shows the suffix large, its meaning in your language, and the full reading table with irregular readings highlighted.

## Meaning and bindings age separately

The meaning of a suffix ("long objects: pencils, bottles, trees") meets you in the acquaintance hand; the regular lesson never quizzes the bare suffix. Instead, the lesson deals the number bindings one by one: the front shows a number with the suffix (8本), you recall its reading, reveal the answer (はっぽん), and rate yourself with the classic **Know / Don't know** buttons, the same as every other card. After each answer the app shows the table of readings for the whole suffix, with the row you just answered accented and irregular readings highlighted. Every binding keeps its own memory, so irregular readings like いっぽん, さんぼん, ろっぽん come back more often than regular ones. Marking a suffix as known skips the meaning and the bindings together.

The regular review asks the number bindings one by one: a number with the suffix on the front, its reading after the reveal, and the classic know / don't-know rating.

## Where the counters come from

Counters arrive with your level: the onboarding import adds the suffixes for your JLPT level and below, and the dedicated counters page lists every suffix with its detail card, so you can browse, add, and review them yourself. Adding a word with a numeral compound (一本) from text also offers the counter as a distinct candidate. If you already know a suffix, mark it as known: both the meaning and the number bindings skip their drills. For accounts created before counters existed, a one-time migration detects the suffixes your vocabulary already met the first time the app starts after the update.

The dataset is maintained as a data file and covers the suffixes taught by common textbooks, grouped by JLPT level by editorial consensus: there is no official JLPT counter list. Glosses are maintained in English, Russian, Korean, and Vietnamese. See [data sources](/docs/data-sources) for provenance.

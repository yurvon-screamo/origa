# Japanese counter words

Japanese counts things with counter suffixes: long objects with 本, flat things with 枚, small animals with 匹, people with 人. Origa treats each suffix as one card and trains two kinds of knowledge inside it.

## One suffix is one card

A counter card carries the suffix itself (what it counts) and the number bindings: the readings of one through ten, the question form 何, and a few fixed forms beyond ten (the days of the month like 二十日, read はつか). You meet the card through the acquaintance stage like any other new card: the presentation slide shows the suffix large, its meaning in your language, and the full reading table with irregular readings highlighted.

## Meaning and bindings age separately

The meaning of a suffix ("long objects: pencils, bottles, trees") and its number bindings (三本 → さんぼん) follow separate review schedules. In a regular review you either rate the meaning with **Know / Don't know**, or the lesson deals a bindings batch: a run of small questions where you see a number with the suffix and pick the correct reading. Each binding remembers its own answers, so irregular readings like いっぽん, さんぼん, ろっぽん come back more often than regular ones, which drop out of rotation as they solidify.

After each answer in a batch the app shows the table of readings for the whole suffix, with the row you just answered highlighted. This is the same table you saw at the first meeting, now used as feedback.

## Where the counters come from

Counters arrive with your level: the onboarding import adds the suffixes for your JLPT level and below, so the acquaintance pool meets them gradually. If you already know a suffix, mark it during onboarding scoring: the meaning skips training, though the number bindings still get their drills. For accounts created before counters existed, Origa detects suffixes that appear in your added words (一本 in your vocabulary brings in 本) and adds those cards alongside the rest of your level.

The dataset is maintained in the repository and covers the suffixes taught by common textbooks, grouped by JLPT level by editorial consensus: there is no official JLPT counter list. Glosses are maintained in English, Russian, Korean, and Vietnamese. See [data sources](/docs/data-sources) for provenance.

## Why some lists say 80 and others 103

You will meet both numbers. Neither is wrong; they count differently.

The JLPT organizers do not publish an official kanji list per level. What circulates are teacher reconstructions, and two conventions dominate. The older one (pre-2010 exam revisions, still common in study guides) assigns about 103 kanji to N5. The newer one splits the standard Jōyō set by usage frequency (the same split used by our dictionary and by reference sites like Kanshudo) and lands on 80 for N5, moving the remaining ~23 characters to the N4 boundary.

Practically: if you learn the 80 below and then continue into [the N4 range](/jlpt/n4/en.pdf), you cover every character the older convention counts as N5, with room to spare. Arguing about the number is less useful than covering both ranges.

## How to learn a kanji list without wasting it

Three rules that hold for any list size:

1. **Frequency first.** The table is sorted by usage, and the top rows carry disproportionate weight: the first 20 characters already appear in most beginner sentences. Front-load them.
2. **Learn kanji inside words.** A character with a reading and no word attached is trivia. The "common words" column is the actual payload: 人 as 人間 and 一人 is knowledge, while 人 alone is a puzzle piece. [The N5 preparation guide](/blog/jlpt-n5-preparation) covers how the vocabulary around these kanji fits the exam.
3. **Review on a schedule, not on willpower.** Spaced repetition beats re-reading the PDF. Origa builds this loop from the material you already read (textbook pages, manga, subtitles), turning encountered kanji into scheduled cards, offline. [How it works](/features) · [download](/download).

## FAQ

### How many kanji are in JLPT N5?

80 by the modern frequency-based split of the Jōyō set (used here); about 103 in older pre-2010 conventions. The exam organizers publish no official per-level list, so both numbers are reconstructions.

### Is this list enough for the exam?

For the kanji section of N5, the 80 characters below plus the N4 boundary range cover everything either convention assigns to the level. Kanji alone is not the exam: vocabulary and listening weigh just as much (see the N5 preparation guide).

### Can I print the list?

Yes. The PDFs above are free, generated from the same data as the table, and updated when the data changes. Landscape A4, one row per kanji.

### What comes after N5?

The N4 range: about 170 more kanji in the same split. Its PDF is in the table above, and the study loop does not change: new characters keep entering through words you meet in real text.
